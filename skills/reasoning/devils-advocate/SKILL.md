---

## Purpose

Given a stated position, decision, or idea, construct the strongest good-faith argument *against* it — even if that argument is ultimately weaker than the case for it. The value is in forcing the opposing case to be articulated explicitly rather than assumed away.

## When to Use

- The user has already decided on something and wants to know what a smart opponent would say against it.
- A group or team seems to be converging on consensus too quickly.
- Before finalizing a decision that's hard to reverse.

This differs from [GrillMe](../grill-me/): GrillMe attacks weaknesses broadly across many angles; Devil's Advocate builds one coherent, sustained counter-position, as if arguing the other side in a debate.

## What to Analyze

1. **Identify the stated position** in one sentence.
2. **Steelman the opposite position** — construct the best version of the counter-argument, not a weak strawman.
3. **Find the strongest supporting evidence or precedent** for the counter-position (similar cases, known failure patterns, opportunity cost).
4. **Anticipate the rebuttal** the original position would offer, and address it directly rather than ignoring it.
5. **State a genuine verdict** at the end: does the counter-argument actually hold up, partially hold up, or fail — don't manufacture false balance.

## Output Format

- One paragraph: the strongest counter-position, stated as a real argument (not "some might say...").
- Supporting points as a short list, each with why it matters.
- A short "anticipated rebuttal and response" section.
- A closing verdict line: how strong is this counter-argument, honestly.

## Avoid

- Strawmanning the opposing case to make it easy to knock down.
- Refusing to give a verdict — "it depends" without a lean is not useful.
- Conflating this with general criticism (see GrillMe) — stay focused on one coherent counter-position.
